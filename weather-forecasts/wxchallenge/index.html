        How to read this page

        Consensus

        A skill-weighted average of the models in the forecast table. Each model's typical error this period is partly taken out (more as scored days add up: 2 days 40%, 5 days 62%, 14 days 82%; lows, whose errors tend to persist, faster: 2 days 67%, 14 days 93%), and models that have been closer to the observations count more. Recent days count somewhat more than older ones, so the blend adapts without a few bad days undoing weeks of record. With no scored days yet at a new city it is a plain average. Models far from the rest (over 8 °F or 10 kt from the middle) are left out, and so are runs over 36 h older than the newest; the line under the table names them each day, along with models not scored yet (used as they are, or with their parent model's record).

        Forecasts and observations

        Each 06Z–06Z day (between the vertical lines) shows the model runs that were out before its 00Z deadline; tomorrow's keep updating until then. Scroll or swipe left for earlier days. Black is observed: every METAR (×: gusts); in the cloud panel the line is the GOES satellite cloud fraction and the dots ASOS sky cover (open: clear; ASOS sees clouds only up to 12,000 ft). Dotted lines are today's newest HRRR, MOS and NBM runs; the dashed grey line is the hourly average of the models switched on (a plain mean, not the consensus). The Dewpoint & RH panels button hides or shows dewpoint and relative humidity below the cloud panel (RH worked out from each temperature and dewpoint; for the ensembles, the mean of their members' RH).

        Model verification

        Each day is scored with the model's last forecast before the 00Z deadline, using WxChallenge-style points (1 per °F, 0.5 per kt, tiered precip). ★ = best model that day. Observed values come from METARs and the NWS climate report until WxChallenge posts its final values for the day (each morning), which then replace them; the Observed cell's tooltip says which, and gives this page's own value where the two differed. Shaded days come before this city's first forecast day: they are hindcasts (the runs available at 23Z the day before), there to give every model a track record from day one. The consensus on those days (italic, r) was not submitted: it is reconstructed the way the consensus works now, using only the days before it. The last column (so far) is today: the forecasts scored against the observations so far. It isn't final (the high or low may not have happened yet), so it is left out of the totals, the averages and the consensus's track record.

        One run per model per day is scored: its last one before the deadline (mostly the 18Z run; 12Z for the IFS and ENS, about 22Z for NBM, 22Z for USL). Score the 12Z and 18Z runs separately gives each model a row per run from the day before instead (12Z and 18Z; USL 12Z and 22Z; NBM also its submitted run; IFS, IFS 9 km and ENS 12Z only, as their 18Z runs come out after the deadline), each with its own points, average and bias, and ranks every run on its own average. Runs the page didn't keep at the time, including the hindcast days, are downloaded again where the archive has them (not the ensembles or USL's runs from before the city). It is for comparing runs only: the normal view, the consensus and its weights don't change.

        Ensembles

        ECMWF IFS ensemble (51 members), NCEP GEFS (31; shown and scored but not part of the consensus), all via Open-Meteo, plus two 3 km precipitation ensembles: REFS (NOAA's new operational short-range ensemble: RRFS, RRFS ensemble and HRRR members from the latest two cycles) and HRRRCast (NOAA GSL's experimental AI emulator of the HRRR, 18 members), for the same 06Z–06Z day. The precip axis is square-root scaled so light amounts aren't squeezed by a few wet members. Probabilities are the share of members reaching each amount. Because of how precip is scored, the members' median is the best single precip value to enter.

        Chance of precipitation

        Chance of at least 0.01" (a trace counts as dry) in each 6-hour block of the 06Z–06Z day and over the whole day. MOS and NBM text guidance (P06) and NBM QMD are calibrated probabilities; the ensembles are the raw share of their members (uncalibrated, and coarse for small ensembles: note the member counts). MOS has 6-hour chances only, so its 24-hour chance is shown as a range: at least the largest block chance, at most the chance if the blocks were independent. None of this goes into the consensus. In the charts, each line holds one source's chance for each 6-hour block (UTC). The table has every number. The NBM QMD 50% level is the amount that minimises the expected WxChallenge precip points.

        Machine-readable version: the forecasts, model verification, scoring rules and today-so-far numbers shown on this page are summarised as plain text at https://jameslarsonclimate.github.io/website/weather-forecasts/data/wxchallenge/brief.md (raw JSON: forecast.json, verification.json, today.json).
